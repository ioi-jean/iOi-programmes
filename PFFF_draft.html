Study design, by age group

Same underlying construct, different instrument for each band — the "next step" is making sure all three map back to the same analytical variable (e.g. well-being, confidence) so results are comparable.

Group 1 — Ages 5–7 Group 2 — Ages 8–10 Group 3 — Ages 11–13
Methodology Proxy-Plus reporting: parents/teachers for baseline developmental data, plus the Mosaic Approach (observations, cameras, child-led tours, drawing) for the child's own perspective. Tablet-based, gamified questionnaires. Independent, mobile-friendly digital surveys or text-message data collection.
Survey design Physical "smiley face" sorting buckets — children drop a token into happy / neutral / sad to rate an activity. Minimal text, 3-point visual Likert scale (stars/emojis, not words). Short and snappy to avoid cognitive fatigue. Standard 5-point Likert scale.
Input style Visual, play-based, proxy survey; short, task-based inputs. Interactive digital survey; gamified, icon scales. Independent digital survey.
Tool to build Proxy survey for Group 1 parents. Emoji-based survey for Group 2. Standard online survey for Group 3.

Recruitment & engagement messaging

Keep it light, fun and engaging — attract all types of parents/carers, lead with the Playing for a Fairer Future logo, and give each audience a reason that speaks to them.

Parents / carers

Take part in a play & imagination project from the iOi!

You'll have the opportunity to play and try new things, be invited to exclusive iOi events and workshops, and feed into a National Lottery funded research project — a 5-year study following children's experiences to understand how play, creativity and imagination help young people grow and thrive. What you'll do: take part in surveys created by children, for children, enjoy exciting resources and activities, and be in with the chance of winning vouchers.

Schools / educators

Take part in a large-scale play project from the iOi!

A 5-year research project following children's experiences over time to understand how play, creativity and imagination improve confidence and skills, in the classroom and across transitional moments. What you'll do: share the opportunity with children and parents/carers, become a school hub for play, imagination and creativity, and open up funded workshops and events for your school and community.

Build-to-launch roadmap

Phase 1 — Ethics, consent & data

Foundations
  • Finalise information sheets and consent form
  • Set up the filing system and data security plan
  • Stand up Monday CRM for the study

Phase 2 — Tool & survey design, pilot testing

Design
  • Draft and create baseline questionnaires, assessment metrics and interview scripts
  • Run a mini-pilot with 2–3 children from each age group we're working with

Phase 3 — Adult engagement & recruitment

Community
  • Liaise directly with connected adults — headteachers, youth club leaders, regional charity coordinators
  • Distribute parent/carer and teacher packs; host brief information sessions on the study's long-term value
  • Roll out the engagement/retention strategy (birthday cards, milestones, impact certificates)

Phase 4 — Field readiness & launch

Launch
  • Set up baseline data collection tracking sheets (Monday / Excel)
  • Train field researchers and child co-researchers
  • Confirm minimum parent/carer recruitment targets are reached
  • Launch the first wave of data collection

Open questions

What are we naming the longitudinal study for maximum external impact?

Under discussion — options include "iOi research" or "Playing for a Fairer Future study."

Are we involving children as co-researchers?

Yes — via the Imagination Council, with training.

Who trains the field researchers and child co-researchers?

To confirm — university partner (UCL) named in early planning notes.

How do we standardise across the three age-group instruments?

Ensure each core construct (e.g. well-being, confidence, reading ability) maps to the same underlying analytical variable so results are comparable across groups.
