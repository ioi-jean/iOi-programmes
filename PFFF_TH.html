Playing for a Fairer Future — Tower Hamlets Longitudinal Study

A working hub for the creation, planning and running of the large-scale longitudinal study behind Playing for a Fairer Future — the National Lottery-funded programme following 50,000 children across five UK regions over five years. Tower Hamlets is where the original Power of Play work began, so this is the natural base for building and testing the study before it rolls out nationally.

Research question & framework

Everything below — naming, consent, survey design, recruitment — is in service of one question, tracked through the same underlying model across all three age groups.

Overarching research question

How does participation in play-based experiences support the development of imagination, agency and belonging towards shaping a fairer future for children over time?

Play → Imagination → Curious / Make / Share / Belong / Challenge → Agency & Belonging → Fairer Futures
Competency “I can” statement Draft survey question
Curious I can wonder Overall, how often have you wondered about things recently?
Make I can create Overall, how capable have you felt of creating something new recently?
Share I can question Overall, how willing have you been to share and discuss ideas recently?
Belong I can explore Overall, how willing have you been to explore people, places, and perspectives recently?
Challenge I can try Overall, how willing have you been to try new things recently?

Standardise across groups: whatever each age band's tool looks like on the surface, make sure the underlying concept it's measuring (well-being, confidence, reading ability, etc.) maps to the same analytical variable, so the three tracks stay comparable over five years.

Planning phases

Five phases from naming the study through to launching the first wave of data collection. Not strictly sequential — Consent and Design can run in parallel — but each needs to be signed off before Launch.

Define

Naming the study and deciding how children are involved in shaping it.

Decisions to make

  • What we're naming the longitudinal study for maximum external impact — options on the table: “iOi research” or “Playing for a Fairer Future study”
  • Whether children are involved as co-researchers

Current thinking

  • Yes to co-researchers — via an Imagination Council, plus training for the children involved
  • Naming decision affects how the study reads to funders, press and partner schools — worth testing both options

Consent

Finalising ethics, consent and data handling before any collection begins.

To finalise

  • Information sheet — separate versions for teachers, parents/carers, and children
  • Consent form
  • Filing system and data security plan
  • Monday.com CRM set up

Watch for

  • Three different audiences need three different reading levels on the information sheet — the children's version especially
  • Data security plan needs to be live before baseline collection starts, not after

Design

Building and pilot-testing the age-banded tools.

To build

  • Baseline questionnaires, assessment metrics or interview scripts for each age group
  • Mini-pilot with 2–3 children per age band

Watch for

  • Pilot before committing — a tool that works for 8–10s on a tablet may not translate to the 5–7s' bucket-sort approach
  • Keep the underlying variable standardised across all three tools (see framework note above)

Recruit

Engaging the adults who make participation possible.

To do

  • Liaise directly with connected adults — headteachers, youth club leaders, regional charity coordinators
  • Distribute parent/carer and teacher packs; host brief information sessions on the long-term value of the study
  • Roll out the engagement strategy (see below) to sustain participation over 5 years

Watch for

  • Keep messaging light, fun and engaging — this is what attracts all types of parents/carers, not just the already-engaged ones
  • Use the Playing for a Fairer Future logo consistently across packs and sessions

Launch

Getting field-ready and opening the first wave of data collection.

To do

  • Set up baseline data collection tracking sheets (Monday/Excel)
  • Train field researchers (UCL) and child co-researchers
  • Confirm minimum parent/carer recruitment targets are reached
  • Launch the first wave of data collection

Watch for

  • This first wave sets the baseline every future year gets compared against — prioritise completeness over speed
  • Confirm co-researcher training has actually landed before they're in the field, not just been delivered

Age-banded survey design

Three tracks, one underlying model. Each is built for how children at that age actually engage — physical and proxy-led for the youngest, gamified and visual in the middle band, independent and digital for the oldest.

Age 5–7 Age 8–10 Age 11–13
Format Visual, play, proxy survey Interactive digital survey Independent digital survey
Approach Short, task-based inputs Gamified, icon scales Standard 5-point Likert

Group 1 — Ages 5–7

Proxy-Plus

Methodology

Proxy-Plus reporting: rely on parents and teachers for baseline developmental data, but capture the child's own perspective using the Mosaic Approach — combining observations, cameras, child-led tours and drawing.

Survey design

Physical “smiley face” sorting buckets — children drop a token into a happy, neutral or sad bucket to rate an activity.

Group 2 — Ages 8–10

Gamified

Methodology

Tablet-based, gamified questionnaires.

Survey design

Minimal text. 3-point visual Likert scales — star ratings or emojis rather than text descriptions. Keep it short and snappy to avoid cognitive fatigue.

Group 3 — Ages 11–13

Independent

Methodology

Independent, mobile-friendly digital surveys, or text-message data collection.

Survey design

Standard 5-point Likert scales.

Next up: design a proxy survey for Group 1 parents, an emoji survey for Group 2, and a standard online survey for Group 3 — then pilot all three before Recruit begins in earnest.
