Where we started

Child voice already happens across the iOi, but mostly in pockets. A two-hour team session grounded the strategy in what we already do. The strategy work set out to make it consistent, visible and measurable.

Imagination Champions

An early co-creation model: 12 children and 5 teachers working with us through 6 touch points per school theme, testing activities and giving feedback on programmes.

Programme-level voice

STEAM Live focus groups and pupil interviews, a teacher group, Imagination Box testing with SEND educators and parents, and co-design in Power of Play.

What the team session surfaced

In a two-hour session to finesse the strategy, the team said time is short (often one-hour activities), delivery dominates conversations, and we need confidence, techniques and capacity to turn what children say into quotes and actions rather than numbers.

The team also asked for clarity on how child voice differs from family voice, how it changes between a lone child and a group, and what to do when children's answers don't match what we expected.

Principles and strategy 2026 to 2029

The vision: every child involved with the iOi has the power, opportunity and confidence to shape the experiences, environments and decisions that affect them, with imagination, fun and play at the core.

Children are experts

Their perspectives are knowledge, not just feedback.

Imagination is a right

A right, not a luxury, whatever a child's background.

Participation leads to influence

Children can see how their ideas shaped decisions.

Inclusive by design

Including socioeconomic disadvantage, SEND, care experience and neurodiversity.

Safe and age-appropriate

Enjoyable, accessible and underpinned by safeguarding.

Co-creation over consultation

Children help define the questions, not just react to answers.

Transparency and accountability

Children hear what was decided, what happened, and why if it couldn't be done.

The strategy has been narrowed to five objectives so it matches the five stages of our pedagogy, rather than adding another framework for staff to carry.

The Imagination Cycle

A child's confidence is their position in the cycle, not a separate measure. When a child reaches Share they return to Wonder, this time helping spark someone else's curiosity. Select a stage to see its objective.

The Imagination Council

A diverse group of children who share ideas, test concepts, co-design experiences and influence decisions. It is the main home for ongoing decision-making in the Challenge stage of the cycle.

Our promise: when you share your ideas with us, we will listen, take them seriously, tell you what happened next and show you how your imagination has helped shape the iOi.

How it will work

Members

12 to 20 children aged about 8 to 13, recruited for diversity of geography, ethnicity, gender, background, SEND, care experience and school setting. Membership rotates.

Core Council

Meets quarterly to advise on key projects, review plans and concepts, and shape strategy.

Project panels

Short-term groups for a festival, exhibition, new programme, research project, communications campaign or even hiring staff.

Meetings

Likely Saturdays, two hours. Welcome and icebreaker, creative warm-up, challenge, action, choices (voting, ranking, debating), then reflect and plan next.

A year with the Council

  1. AutumnRecruit and onboard members.
  2. WinterExplore priorities and identify key areas of focus.
  3. SpringCo-design projects and give feedback on developing initiatives.
  4. SummerCelebrate achievements, publish the impact report, hold a graduation.

Designed with children first

Before the Council is finalised, we are running a two-hour design workshop with 8 to 15 children. They answer why the Council should exist, what it should do, who needs a seat, what makes children feel heard, and what would make them want to join. Activities include If I Could, the Superpower Challenge, a Dream Council Design Studio, the Missing Voices Map, the Listening Ladder and a Lego prototype challenge. The output is a short report: children's vision, priority topics, how it should work, Council promises and top recommendations. We then go back and show children exactly what we included.

Recruitment and recognition

  • Initial strands through Bonner, Marner and Cayley schools, coordinated with the longitudinal study so we don't overload senior leaders and teachers.
  • Possible wider recruitment via Instagram and LinkedIn, with a parent and carer form covering consent.
  • Welcome kit, membership card and lanyard, an option to opt in for a second year, and a graduation with certificate or badge.
  • Safeguarding set up before the first meeting: boundaries, behaviour guidance, ratios and risk assessment.

Putting it into action: three phases

The team session mapped what is already happening, then sorted new ideas into three phases that grow from our own practice towards something other organisations can use.

Already happening

  • A facilitator guide for the Imagination Cycle, with 3 to 5 questions per stage and behaviours to watch for
  • A confidence cycle survey, tested in Young Makers in the Docks
  • An Imagination Passport booklet for children to track their voice, also being tested there
  • Belfast work and Imagination Council recruitment at Bonner

Phase 1: build the basics

  • A toolkit of methods: maps, signs, drawing, stamps
  • A physical kit for Harrison College students
  • Training peer researchers as part of Playing for a Fairer Future
  • Trialling child voice strategies in iOi programmes
  • A scenario guide for when a child's answer doesn't match the session

Phase 2: pilot and share

  • 3 to 5 small pilots that vary school setting, geography, recruitment method and demographics
  • iOi play researchers and play ambassadors: children confident enough to lead sessions elsewhere
  • A shareable framework and toolkit for involving children in programmes
  • A purchasable child voice kit for educators, charities and organisations

Phase 3: spread the expertise

  • A core and flexible toolkit others can replicate or adapt
  • Best practice guidance and a one-page policy brief
  • An annual showcase presenting findings, closing the loop publicly
  • Licensing or train-the-trainer so the framework can scale beyond iOi delivery
  • Pilot learnings repackaged into case studies

Principles from the session: track and test rather than only measure outcomes, keep data gathering light (for example children's own evidence of impact, such as a council zine, video or podcast), and plan for prep time, recruiting time, relationship building and costs. In Playing for a Fairer Future, advocacy, the Imagination Council test and the longitudinal study are in scope; a large-scale test and a national test are not.

Everyday techniques

  • Voting: stickers, Menti, movement response games
  • Creative feedback: crafts, writing on large sheets, mood boards, feedback bunting, storytelling dice, hands-on prototyping
  • Small group discussion: for example, speaking with one to three families works well in the London Museum's New Museum Family Panel

Questions to answer as we evaluate

  • Who decides what success looks like?
  • When and how do young people shape evaluation questions or methods?
  • How are findings determined and shared with young people?
  • What decisions have changed as a result?
  • How clear is it that young people's views have been acted upon?

Funding and wider links

Playing for a Fairer Future (a five-year project) carries much of the strategy: children as co-creators in every location, targeted work with underserved communities, evidence for the National Lottery, and the longitudinal study. The Council may involve children as co-researchers in that study.

Gaps that need other funding:

  • Embedding org-wide: framework, impact assessments, staff toolkits, training and KPIs.
  • The Council itself: accessibility support, a future coordinator, recruitment and recognition budgets.
  • Children's Imagination Report: larger-scale research and evaluation.
  • Opportunities: participation and children's rights funders, a sellable child voice toolkit and training for schools and charities, and a university research partner to validate our approach.

An idea under discussion is an Imagination Influence Index: children answer five questions (did adults listen, did my ideas shape something, can I see the evidence, can my imagination change things, would I help again) combined into one annual headline measure.
